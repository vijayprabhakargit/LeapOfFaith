## What was covered

- Latitude and longitude as angular coordinates, with the crucial asymmetry that latitude has a natural zero and longitude does not.
- Parallels versus meridians, and the variation in the length of a degree of longitude from about 111 km at the Equator to about 56 km at 60°N.
- Great circles and small circles, with the Equator as the only great-circle parallel.
- The five major circles (Equator, tropics at 23½°, polar circles at 66½°) derived from the Earth's axial tilt, and their consequences for insolation, climate belts, Coriolis strength and day length.
- Longitude as time: 15° = 1 hour, 1° = 4 minutes, 1′ of arc = 4 seconds, with Delhi's ~21-minute local lag behind IST as the worked Indian example.
- Local versus standard time, the 1884 International Meridian Conference and the choice of Greenwich, and why time-zone boundaries are political rather than geometric.
- India's standard meridian at 82°30′E (UTC+5:30), the arithmetic behind the half-hour offset, and the two-time-zone debate.
- The International Date Line: its zig-zag, the Kiribati (1995) and Samoa (2011) adjustments, and the westward-add / eastward-subtract rule.
