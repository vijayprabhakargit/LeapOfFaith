## What was covered

- Wegener's continental drift: Pangaea, Panthalassa, Laurasia, Gondwanaland and Tethys; the 1912 papers and the 1915 book.
- The four evidence types: jigsaw fit (refined by Bullard, Everett and Smith, 1965, at the 500-fathom contour), fossils (Glossopteris, Mesosaurus, Lystrosaurus, Cynognathus), matching rock belts (Ghana–Brazil, Caledonian–Appalachian) and palaeoclimate (tillites in India, coal in Antarctica, evaporites in the Sahara).
- Why drift was rejected: no adequate mechanism (pole-fleeing force and tidal drag, dismissed by Jeffreys), the fixist tradition built on isostasy, and the outsider status of Wegener.
- The 1960s revolution: wartime echo sounding, Hess and the guyots, Marie Tharp and Bruce Heezen's Great Global Rift, Hess's 1962 sea-floor spreading, and Vine–Matthews–Morley magnetic striping in 1963.
- The clinching evidence: ocean floor nowhere older than about 180–200 million years, age increasing away from ridges, confirmed by deep-sea drilling from 1968.
- The three plate boundaries: divergent (ridge, rift, East African Rift, Iceland), convergent (ocean–ocean, ocean–continent, continent–continent; trench, island arc, volcanic arc, Himalaya) and transform (San Andreas, Anatolian, Chaman).
- Why earthquakes and volcanoes cluster at margins, and the Ring of Fire.
- Mantle convection (Holmes, 1929), slab pull versus ridge push versus basal drag, hotspots (Wilson, 1963; Hawaii–Emperor, Reunion–Deccan, Kerguelen–Rajmahal) and the Wilson cycle.

## Notes for the next teacher

This lesson deliberately paid off the lithosphere/asthenosphere distinction from Lesson 5 in its opening and again in the convection section — the bridge worked and should be reused. The isostasy callback in the 'why the world said no' section is worth keeping: it explains why fixism was rational, not stupid. The Inge Lehmann thread was extended to Marie Tharp, which gives the series a running women-in-science motif; the next teacher can reuse both. Three new figures were built. 'pangaea-evidence' is reusable for Gondwana, for the Damodar coalfields and for any question on palaeoclimatic evidence. 'magnetic-striping' is reusable for palaeomagnetism, for the geodynamo and magnetic field reversal questions, and pairs naturally with the 'earth-interior' figure from Lesson 5. 'plate-boundaries' is the master boundary figure and should be reused in the earthquake lesson (for the Benioff zone and for the depth of quakes at each margin) and in the volcano lesson. Deliberately NOT done here: the Benioff zone was named only in passing and deserves full treatment next time; the Wilson cycle was introduced but not developed; and no Indian seismic zone detail was given, since that belongs to the earthquake lesson. The 'map as argument' stance from Lesson 1 was not re-established, and the Coriolis preview from Lesson 4 remains unpaid — it still belongs to atmospheric circulation.
