## What was covered

- Focus and hypocentre, epicentre, fault plane and focal depth; elastic-rebound theory and the 1906 San Francisco fence-line surveys.
- Seismic waves: P, S, Love and Rayleigh; the S-wave and P-wave shadow zones as evidence for the liquid outer core (callback to the interior lesson); the seismograph and epicentre location from the P–S time gap.
- Magnitude versus intensity; the Richter scale and its saturation; moment magnitude (Mw); Modified Mercalli and MSK intensity; site amplification (Ahmedabad 2001, Kathmandu 2015).
- Depth classification — shallow (0–70 km), intermediate (70–300 km), deep (300–700 km); the Wadati–Benioff zone and why the Himalaya produces only shallow quakes; global distribution and the Ring of Fire.
- Tsunami generation — vertical sea-floor displacement, long wavelength, jet speed in deep water, shoaling at the coast; the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami and the INCOIS warning centre at Hyderabad.
- India's seismic zones II–V (BIS IS 1893, based on MSK intensity), the central seismic gap, and the case studies of Latur 1993, Bhuj 2001 and Nepal 2015.
