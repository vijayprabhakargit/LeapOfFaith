## What was covered

- The problem of indirect knowledge: Kola borehole at 12.26 km against a 6,371 km radius; the four witnesses (density, seismic waves, gravity, meteorites).
- The density argument: crustal rock 2.7–3.0 g/cm³ against a mean Earth density of about 5.5 g/cm³.
- Chemical layering: oceanic crust (5–10 km, basaltic, ~3.0) versus continental crust (30–70 km, granitic, ~2.7); SiAl and SiMa; mantle to 2,900 km, peridotite, solid but plastic; core as NIFE, outer liquid, inner solid.
- Discontinuities: Moho (crust–mantle), Gutenberg (mantle–core), Lehmann (outer–inner core), with Conrad mentioned only in passing.
- Seismic waves: P (longitudinal, fastest, all media), S (transverse, solids only), L (surface, slowest, most destructive).
- Shadow zones: S-wave shadow beyond 103°; P-wave shadow 103°–142°; P waves returning beyond 142° via inner-core refraction. The nesting of the P shadow inside the S shadow was emphasised.
- Why the outer core is liquid and the inner core solid: pressure at the inner-core boundary around 330 GPa raising the melting point of iron above the ambient temperature; the geodynamo and the growing inner core.
- Lithosphere versus asthenosphere as a mechanical rather than chemical division; the low-velocity zone; the lithosphere as the plate.
- Isostasy: Airy (uniform density, variable thickness, roots) versus Pratt (variable density, uniform depth of compensation); Himalayan crustal root, Ganga plain flexure, Peninsular shield rigidity, post-glacial rebound in Scandinavia and Canada.
- The geoid: equipotential surface at mean sea level, departures of up to about ±100 m from the WGS84 ellipsoid, orthometric versus ellipsoidal heights, and the Indian Ocean Geoid Low at roughly 106 m.

'isostasy-airy' is reusable for the Himalaya, for the Ganga basin, for post-glacial rebound, for gravity anomalies and for the broader theme of crustal compensation.
